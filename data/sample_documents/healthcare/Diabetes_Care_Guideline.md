# Clinical Guideline: Management of Type 2 Diabetes in Adults (CG-END-002)

## 1. Scope

This guideline (CG-END-002, version 3.2, approved by the Department of Endocrinology on 9 January 2026) applies to adults aged 18 years and above with type 2 diabetes attending outpatient clinics and admitted to general wards. It does not cover pregnancy, type 1 diabetes or children, for which separate guidelines exist.

## 2. Diagnosis

Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed when any one of the following is confirmed on repeat testing: fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test, or HbA1c of 6.5% or higher measured by an NGSP-certified method.

## 3. Treatment Targets

| Parameter | Target for most adults | Older or frail adults |
|---|---|---|
| HbA1c | Below 7.0% | Below 8.0% |
| Fasting capillary glucose | 80 - 130 mg/dL | 90 - 150 mg/dL |
| Blood pressure | Below 130/80 mmHg | Below 140/90 mmHg |
| LDL cholesterol | Below 100 mg/dL (below 70 if cardiovascular disease) | Individualised |

## 4. Monitoring Schedule

- HbA1c every 3 months until the patient reaches the target, then every 6 months if stable.
- Retinal screening (dilated fundus examination or fundus photography) annually.
- Comprehensive foot examination annually, and at every visit for patients with neuropathy or prior ulcers.
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and eGFR annually.
- Lipid profile annually.

## 5. Pharmacological Therapy

Metformin is the first-line agent unless contraindicated. Start with 500 mg once daily with the evening meal and increase by 500 mg every 1 to 2 weeks to a usual maximum of 2,000 mg per day. Metformin is contraindicated when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 and the dose should be halved when eGFR is between 30 and 45. For patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, add an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist regardless of HbA1c. Insulin is started when HbA1c remains above 10% or symptoms of severe hyperglycaemia are present.

## 6. Hypoglycaemia

Hypoglycaemia is a blood glucose below 70 mg/dL. Conscious patients should follow the rule of 15: take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate (for example 3 glucose tablets or 150 mL of fruit juice), recheck the blood glucose after 15 minutes, and repeat if it is still below 70 mg/dL. Once glucose is normal, a snack containing complex carbohydrate should be taken. Unconscious patients must receive intravenous 25% dextrose or intramuscular glucagon 1 mg and never anything by mouth.

## 7. Lifestyle

All patients are referred to the diabetes educator at diagnosis. Recommended physical activity is at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity exercise spread over at least 3 days, with no more than 2 consecutive days without activity. Patients who are overweight should aim for a 5 to 7 percent reduction in body weight.

## 8. Referral Criteria

- Refer to ophthalmology for any diabetic retinopathy beyond mild non-proliferative changes.
- Refer to the diabetic foot clinic within 24 hours for any active foot ulcer.
- Refer to nephrology if eGFR falls below 30 or declines by more than 25% within a year.
